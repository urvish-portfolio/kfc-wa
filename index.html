10:30 · Before the doors openOperational Excellence

Walk the travel path, then walk it again

The travel path is a fixed walk past 13 checkpoints, from the host stand through the kitchen to the chillers, storage and staff room. At Shake Shack Birmingham I walked it every 90 minutes to two hours, all day. A missing sauce or a fridge running warm is cheap to fix at 10:30 and expensive at 12:30.

  • NSF food safety audit, March 2025: 98.71%, with no critical findings. NSF is an independent food-safety auditor.
  • Full operations audit in my last week at Birmingham: 99.9%, with the inspector's note "A near perfectly managed Shack!"
  • 5-star EHO rating. EHO is the UK council food-hygiene inspection, scored out of 5.
11:00 · The first forecastOwnership

Count what tonight needs while there is time to fix it

After a huge trading day at Birmingham, the next delivery arrived short. At 11:00 my forecast showed we would run out of beef patties by about 18:00 and buns an hour later. Friends at other Shake Shack sites sent buns. Nobody could deliver beef. So I called another manager in early to run the floor and drove about an hour and a half each way to collect patties from another store. I was back before 18:00 with 15 patties left, and we traded normally until the 22:00 close.

  • Set the daily and weekly sales forecasts myself, then planned labour, prep and ordering to them.
  • Weekly orders across 11 suppliers and about 180 lines, with every delivery checked against its purchase order and shortages chased for credit.
12:00 · Lunch peakCustomer

Keep service on time when the plan breaks at the back door

International Burger Day 2025 meant buy-one-get-one-free on every burger, and a queue had formed behind the shutter by 10:30. At 11:00, as we opened, a delivery worth about £4,000 arrived, chilled and frozen stock that could not wait. I moved the two team members on Expo, the station where every order is checked and handed over, to put it away. Then I ran Expo alone with 15 to 20 orders on the screen. Most of the team could work most stations, so people came across to help whenever their own station allowed. We took about £11,000 that day, roughly double a normal day.

  • Likelihood to Return in guest feedback rose from 8.1 to 9.3.
  • Orders delivered in under 7 minutes.
  • Ran the fry and chicken station myself and trained the team on it.
15:00 · The afternoon lullPeople at the Heart

Breaks happen, even on the busiest day

On Burger Day, once the queue eased after 15:00, I covered the team's breaks myself, two people at a time. Under-18s were on my rosters at Greggs, Fresh Bake and Shake Shack, and their hours and breaks were set around the legal limits for young workers.

One colleague at Birmingham was struggling with the pace, and I came close to letting him go in his probation. After a shift we talked as friends, and he told me about a condition I hadn't known about. He showed me how to hand him tasks in a way that worked for him. Two months later he was Employee of the Month.

  • Hired 26 people myself at Birmingham. None of them resigned.
  • Casework handled through Croner, a UK employment-law advice service, and a briefing before every shift so each person knew the day's purpose.
22:00 · Close and countInnovation Mindset

Read the stock count before reacting to it

One August 2025 count at Birmingham showed stock about £4,000 below where it should have been, with chicken bites the largest line. I compared it with the previous period, when the stock system had been down and the count had swung the other way. Across the two periods the real gap was under £40, and chicken bites was the one line still worth investigating.

At Greggs the same habit led to new ways of working. We put four of each bestselling sandwich on display first thing, baked savouries in small batches through the day, and reset ordering to the real sales pattern. Sales rose 20% and waste fell 30%, with no extra resources.

  • Wrote the cost-of-goods report twice a month and reviewed the site P&L every period.
  • Challenged delivery-app refunds line by line and recovered 70% of what Deliveroo refunded from May to July 2025.
22:30 · HandoverPositive Impact

Turn a fix into something the team runs without you

At Fresh Bake, a couple who could neither hear nor speak, both allergic to dairy, were struggling to order. I gave them pen and paper, then showed them each ingredient one by one: an iced latte with oat milk and an iced mocha with almond milk. After their second visit I put a note with their order on the counter and briefed the team, so anyone on shift could serve them. On my last day they cried and took a photo with me.

  • Co-developed the allergen guide covering 85 products and the 14 regulated allergens.
  • The opening and closing routines I co-developed for that launch are still in use, as far as I know.

Labour is the lever

Removing junior rates for 18 to 20-year-olds is expected to cost about A$1m in FY27, rising to about A$20m a year by FY31. That is roughly 8% of FY26 KFC Australia EBITDA, so rostering skill now shows up in the margin.

Growth is in-restaurant

With same-store sales up 4% in the first eight weeks of FY27, Kwench rolling out, and later trading hours, much of the next gain will come from restaurants already open.
